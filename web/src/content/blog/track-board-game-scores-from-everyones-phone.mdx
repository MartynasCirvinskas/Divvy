Every regular game-night group has a scorekeeper. Usually it's whoever has the neatest handwriting, or whoever insists most loudly that they're good at mental arithmetic. Everyone else has to trust that the numbers are right — or wait for their turn to squint at a paper scoresheet across the table.

When someone leaves the room or reaches for a snack, the record goes with them. When the game runs long and handwriting degrades, you're guessing at the final round. And if you want to compare this session's scores to last month's game, good luck finding that crumpled notepad.

This article is for game-night regulars who want a better system — one where every player has their own view of the scoreboard on their own phone, with no single point of failure and no phone-passing required.

## Why passing one phone around doesn't work

The passed-phone model has three practical problems.

First, it creates a bottleneck. Someone's trying to enter their score, but the phone is on the other side of the table while two people argue about whether a rule was applied correctly. The score doesn't get logged until the conversation ends.

Second, it concentrates trust. If one person is controlling the scoreboard, everyone else has to either watch closely or accept the numbers. In competitive groups, this creates friction even when nothing is wrong.

Third, it breaks flow. A good game night has a rhythm — deal, play, score, repeat. Every time the phone makes a lap around the table, that rhythm breaks. People check their notifications. Someone gets distracted. The game stalls.

A shared scoreboard that everyone can read from their own phone solves all three problems at once.

## Setting up for game night

Getting everyone connected is the fastest part of the setup. The host creates a group (or uses an existing one), shares the 6-letter code or the join link, and players open EvenJar and enter the code. Each person picks a display name — useful for games where you want to use character names or team names rather than real names.

From there, the host sets up the scoreboard for the session: name the game, add the players, and you're ready to go.

A few practical tips for smooth setup:

**Do it before you sit down.** Spend two minutes getting everyone connected while people are still arriving, drinks in hand. By the time the game is set up on the table, the digital scoreboard is ready too.

**Use a display name that matches how you're playing.** In a team game, use team names. In a game with hidden roles, use character names. It makes the scoreboard feel like part of the game rather than a separate bookkeeping tool.

**Keep EvenJar open during play.** Most phones will dim the screen after a minute of inactivity. Ask everyone to keep the app open so they can glance at the scores between rounds without unlocking their phone.
